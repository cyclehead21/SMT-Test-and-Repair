# Air Bubbles in the SMT System

Written by Cyclehead21@gmail.com

Feel free to share, copy, and duplicate

Just give me a little credit

If you rebuild a GSA or HPU, it is possible that air will be left in the hydraulic system. If so, the air bubble usually finds its way back to the HPU reservoir during normal operation. But not always!

An air bubble in the system can cause a wide variety of problems. Thankfully they are short lived, and usually (but not always) will clear themselves with a simple reset (self relearn, or just wait 30 minutes and the problem will disappear)

I learned that LuK had a practice of vacuum bleeding the system during manufacture. They would apply a vacuum pump to the HPU reservoir to evacuate air from the whole system. They (somehow) isolated the clutch system since the clutch piston seal cannot withstand negative pressures.

I have experimented using an air conditioning system vacuum pump to pull a hard vacuum on the plastic reservoir tank. It seems to work well!

[https://youtube.com/shorts/J2VwcwG-a3Y?si=wVj_bHnmgxhOi-UF](https://youtube.com/shorts/J2VwcwG-a3Y?si=wVj_bHnmgxhOi-UF)

## Symptoms

Symptoms of an air bubble can range from SMT system shutdown, to getting stuck in a gear, to losing a gear, and possibly others.

## Details

I believe that bubbles cause trouble particularly for the operation of the four solenoids. If a bubble disrupts behavior of a solenoid, it can prevent operation of the clutch, or of a shift or select function. The system will very quickly recognize that a command has not executed, and likely throw an SMT system error code. Or it can declare “fatal error” and cut fuel supply to the engine.

## Resolution

An air bubble may resolve itself after 30 minutes or so. Just get everything into neutral and try restarting the car. Other times you may need to try manually cycling the shifter shaft through the gears to help force the air bubble to move. Alternatively it may help to run the HPU and shift through the gears (if possible). You can switch the ignition on (engine off) and cycle through R,N,1 and 2 while the car is stationary.

## Examples

### Flameout

I rebuilt the GSA in my SMT daily driver. After three days of driving (probably 100 miles or more) the system quit shifting. The red gear light illuminated and I coasted into a neighborhood park. I disconnected the battery, waited 20 minutes. Upon reconnection everything had returned to normal. It never happened again with that GSA.

### Stuck in 4th gear!

After removing my GSA (to donate parts to another car) I drove without problems for 60 miles. Upon returning home I found I could not shift out of 4th gear. So I kept moving until I got to my garage. When I stopped and put the car in neutral, the engine shut off - TCU fatal error had cut fuel supply. The next day I ran a full techstream relearn and that was the end of the problems. It did not return with that GSA.

### Can’t find Reverse!

Video [https://youtube.com/shorts/NCUmo8BoBo0?si=Ta8bD8xoGQo_qkW0](https://youtube.com/shorts/NCUmo8BoBo0?si=Ta8bD8xoGQo_qkW0)

I rebuilt another GSA, and swapped it into my daily driver (it has become a test mule). It shifted fine for four days while I made frequent short trips around the neighborhood. Then on the 5th day it could not find reverse. The green neutral light would flash, and the shift shaft would cycle repeatedly into 5th gear. The complete wrong end of the gearshift pattern!! I thought I must have bent a shifter link, or damaged a GSA actuator. I fought with the shifter - trying to manually shift into reverse, for about two days. I verified the neutral and reverse switches were all functioning correctly. Finally in desperation I shifted the car into reverse manually, and forced the cabin shifter into reverse and started the car. It showed reverse on the dashboard! From then on, the car has shifted perfectly.

### Another flameout

I rebuilt a GSA on an SMT Spyder from Tennessee. I drove the car around the neighborhood for 2 weeks - many short trips to exercise the GSA. The owner picked up the car and drove towards their home in Tennessee. 2 hours later they called - the car was stuck at a rest stop 60 miles away. All the warning lights were illuminated, and it would not shift into any gears! I told them I would retrieve the car and drove to find it. When I arrived, the car started fine, shifted fine and all the warning lights were extinguished. Apparently the 2 hour “rest” was sufficient to resolve the air bubble. I drove it 60 miles home without any problems.

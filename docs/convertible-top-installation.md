### My installation Comments:

Notes:

I disagree with installation steps 10 and 11 below (sequence). It appears they have you glue the listing flap onto bow #2 BEFORE you hook the header bow pocket over the header bow (#1). This limits the amount of material you can stretch and makes it very (very) difficult to stretch the header bow pocket into place. Plus it prevents the material from finding its own position by stretching between the header and bow #4. The listing flap on bow #2 should be attached AFTER you successfully attach the material to the header bow.

## 6. Robbins Installation Instructions

Comments:

DIY: Instructions caution strongly against DIY installation. I disagree. The Robbins/Sierra tops seem to be very precisely manufactured, with all the mounting holes precision located, so none (almost) of the installation is simply attaching things in the proper sequence. There are practically no “adjustments” or installation decisions. You simply put screws in the holes provided. The ONLY exception is the side material under the forward trim. Robbins has ceased stitching in a plastic edge with pre-punched holes. Instead they provide a flap of material that you must trim, and poke your own holes. There is very little risk of screwing this up, as the hole locations are not critical to proper installation and operation of the top.

Warranty: Note that DIY installation means you get zero warranty coverage. Robbins especially is focused on “approved installer” support. They will not sell their tops directly to the public - only to retailers and installers. If you have any warranty/quality problems, they will deny your complaint if you didn’t use a professional installer.

![Robbins installation instructions, source page image 1](../images/convertible-top-installation/59-robbins-source-page-01.png)

Panel #2 - the plastic strip (shown) is sewn to the top on original Toyota top. However this strip is omitted on Robbins/Sierra top. They only provide a flap of material. You must trim the excess on the lower edge, and punch your own holes.

Panel #5: the spring included with the Robbins/Sierra top will not work with the Toyota screw & captive washer. The washer is too big. Go find a short M8x1 bolt. I found one with a round head (recessed hex) to use instead of the factory screw.

Panel #6 - don’t go crazy bending the little tab. Just pry it a tiny bit upward to release the cable.

![Robbins installation instructions, source page image 2](../images/convertible-top-installation/60-robbins-source-page-02.png)

Panel #7: Don’t pry the metal strip very far. About ⅛ inch opening should be sufficient to tickle the plastic strip loose. Do not just yank on the material! It is held by a stitched plastic strip that acts like a barb. You push the strip downwards and aft to disengage it. A small 90 degree pick can help get it started.

Panel #8: KEEP the cable and the two springs. Do not throw them out with your old top.

Panel #9: Keep the metal clip from both sides. I had to elongate one of the holes since the new top had slightly wider spacing.

Panel #11: I suppose it’s possible to leave the big cardboard piece in the car, as they show in the pictures. I haven’t tried it that way. I removed one seat from the car for access. Pull the large cardboard deck cover from one side and take it out beneath the big metal cross bar, where the seat used to be. Throw an old towel on the metal shelf to keep from scratching your new glass during installation.

Panel #12: There is one bolt hidden at the very front of this bracket. It is accessible with the top stowed/folded. See the inset photo, at the far right/top corner.

![Robbins installation instructions, source page image 3](../images/convertible-top-installation/61-robbins-source-page-03.png)

Panel #16: Clean out the crap, and look over the vinyl “rain bucket” for cracks or splits. If it’s damaged, you want to patch it up now.

Panel #17: The rivets are not a big deal. Robbins/Sierra include some fancy “click” rivets with large heads on both sides. They (later) say to set the rivets using a hammer. That doesn’t work - it only smashes them and they fall apart. I only had success by using a rivet press (Cheap chinese tool). Overall, though these rivets only serve one function, to hold the floppy pieces together while you carry it back to the car. Once the top is bolted into the car, these rivets serve no purpose whatsoever. So you can decide if you want to fight with them…or not. Either way you must drill out the old rivets and transfer the heavy plastic rain gutter to the new top installation. Edit: most recently I have quit removing the heavy plastic gutter at all. Leave it in the car! Pull away the inner flap and tuck the new fabric onto the studs. Then push the flap back into place. Avoid the hassle of removing the large plastic channel, and the hassle with rivets.

Panel #18: This is the “ear strap”. I think it actually causes more wear to the top. So I leave mine off the car, and let the “ears” stick up. You can fold the “ears” by hand if you want to look tidy for a car show.

![Robbins installation instructions, source page image 4](../images/convertible-top-installation/62-robbins-source-page-04.png)

Panel #5: This shows the “hidden” bolt at the top left of the panel. The quarter bracket is curved, so it’s best to install the aft most bolts first, then push the curved piece over the rest of the studs. If you install the forward studs first, the aft ones will be really hard to align.

Panel #1: Closing rivets with a steel hammer does not work. I used a heavy rivet/eyelet squeeze tool.

Panel #6: Photos are old. Robbins/Sierra no longer stitches the plastic trim piece shown under the installer’s thumb. All you get is a flap of material that is too wide. You must trim the bottom edge (about ¼ inch) and poke your own holes. I disagree with sequence for this step. I would NOT attach this flap until AFTER you have the top tensioned by mounting the material to the header bow. (After Panel #11) Just pull the material snug since it establishes some tension side-to-side.

Panel #6: The spring provided will not work with the salvaged screw and captive washer. The spring end is too short. You need to either cut the washer off, or find another screw.

![Robbins installation instructions, source page image 5](../images/convertible-top-installation/63-robbins-source-page-05.png)

Panel #7: I have never seen a spyder frame that uses the metal plate shown inset 6a. This means the big elastic band is superfluous.

Panel #9: salvage the thin metal clip shown from your old top.

Panel #10: I would delay this step until AFTER panel #11. In fact this should probably be about the last step in the entire mounting process. This listing strip keeps the top from bubbling up like a balloon at highway speeds (airfoil negative pressure). If you attach it before the top is tensioned, you will tack the top to the frame in a relaxed or pre-loaded position. Not good. I glued the listing strip to the frame after the top was completely attached, and the ragtop latches were pulled completely shut. (so the top material is fully tensioned) THEN, paint contact cement onto the flaps and frame, let them dry and press them into position. Yeah a spray gun shown in the photo is pretty ridiculous. Just use a little brush. COVER the console, seats and carpet with old towels because contact cement will drip and ruin things!

Panel #12: I wouldn’t bother hammering the strip closed. Press it tight with your fingers if you must. I just leave mine loose as it was when I tucked the edge inside. I’ve never had it fall out. And the next person that installs a new top on your car will thank you for not fatiguing the metal lip and cracking it.

![Robbins installation instructions, source page image 6](../images/convertible-top-installation/64-robbins-source-page-06.png)

Panel #13: I don’t think glue is needed here. There are SIX screws to hold the material in place, plus being clamped under the stainless steel trim piece. Just poke the six holes and screw on the cover. It will never pull loose. (And the next person that installs a new top on your car will thank you)

Panel#16: Note there is only one tiny screw holding the forward material lip. In the picture, the material is nicely folded over the header bow. Yours (and mine) will stay where it belongs at the tiny-screw location, but it will pull away and “unwrap” before it gets to the header bow strip where the rest of the screws are. This makes the forward seam really ugly and likely creates an air/water leak path. So…I drilled a small hole into the metal frame and added one more small screw to hold the material. (Located where the photo-guy’s three fingers are.) Be careful NOT to let your drill bit poke the metal skin as you finish the hole. The drill point will dent the smooth surface above, and it will show through as an ugly pimple.

![Robbins installation instructions, source page image 7](../images/convertible-top-installation/65-robbins-source-page-07.png)

![Robbins installation instructions, source page image 8](../images/convertible-top-installation/66-robbins-source-page-08.png)

## 7. TIPS with Photos:

In no particular sequence. These photos show details mentioned in the Robbins tips above.

Remove both seats for better access.

Remove the large cardboard firewall cover also. (Shortcut: You can omit this step by simply bending the top flange of the cardboard cover away to expose the 10mm nuts)

Shortcut: It’s seldom worth saving your old top, so there’s no reason to work so hard to preserve it during removal. Save time by slicing the old top around the entire back of the car (below the glass). This will allow quick access to the 10mm nuts for removal. BE CAREFUL not to slice through the nylon straps that run diagonally on both sides.

![Convertible top opened for removal and access](../images/convertible-top-installation/01-top-removal-overview.jpg)

One tip (without photos) - My Robbins top came with a bag of “click” rivets. They were too short, and they wouldn’t “click” together like they are supposed to. I ordered some longer click style rivets, but had to use a heavy rivet-set tool to get them to click. All of this to say - the rivets are NOT needed. They merely hold the heavy plastic “U” trough to the ragtop material as you position the top in the car. Once it is pushed over the threaded studs, the rivets do absolutely nothing. If you omit the rivets, make sure you get the ragtop material positioned inside the heavy “U” plastic trough. The trough carries the water over to the sides, where the drains are.

Shortcut: The last top I installed, I skipped the rivets. They don’t serve any purpose once the top is installed. They are intended to make it easier for you to carry the top and drain troughs to the car, but that is also wasted effort. I opened up the “U” channel with my fingers and removed the old top - leaving the “U” channel in the car. When you put the new top in the car, open the “U” channel with your fingers and push the top over the bolt studs, fold the other flange of the “U” channel back over the studs and you’re done. This saves quite a bit of time if (as the photo above shows) you cut the old top for easy access. No need to remove the large cardboard cover.

All of the holes across the back of the cabin are pre-punched. However this one wasn’t cut through all the layers. Simple fix. This hole is not punched through, on ALL Robbins, Sierra and DiamondMall tops that I have installed.

![Pre-punched top attachment hole not cut through every layer](../images/convertible-top-installation/67-incompletely-punched-hole.jpg)

This metal clip is transferred from your old top to the new top. Robbins hole spacing is slightly larger, so you’ll need to elongate the hole in the clip a little bit. (Same on the “DiamondMall”, Sierra and GAHH tops - which are all made at the same factory as Robbins)

![Metal clip transferred from the old top](../images/convertible-top-installation/68-transferred-metal-clip.jpg)

I added a small sheetmetal screw on each side to keep the front seam from pulling away from the header bow. (Circled in green) BE CAREFUL NOT to drill too deep and hit the smooth metal upper skin. It will make an ugly pimple. Select a short sheetmetal screw with a button head - about ¼ inch length.

![Additional header-bow screw location circled in green](../images/convertible-top-installation/69-added-header-screw.jpg)

These are the unfinished side flaps. As in the picture, I believe you should install these AFTER the top is tensioned and hooked over the header bow. There’s no reason to pull them very tight. It helps to thread the tiny sheetmetal screws into the fabric first, then hunt for the hole in the sheetmetal. I see no reason to glue them as instructions say. If you install the forwardmost screw first, then you can use some pliers to tug the fabric tight for the middle and aftmost screws.

![Unfinished side material flap](../images/convertible-top-installation/70-unfinished-side-flap.jpg)

This is the “bubble top preventer” listing strip. I brush contact cement onto the fabric and the metal frame. Let both surfaces dry until they aren’t shiny/wet any more. Then press them together. Try to pull them very tight before you press them down. NOTICE that the top is pre-tensioned by having everything else installed, and the ragtop latches secured and latched. (Tight tight tight!). Some of the instructions above say to glue this flap before you tension the outer fabric. I DO NOT think that’s a good idea to attach this flap before the top material is in its final, tensioned position.

![Listing strip attachment with the top tensioned](../images/convertible-top-installation/71-listing-strip.jpg)

Get the fabric flap and the nylon strap routed as shown. (The nylon strap goes THROUGH the eye, sorry this is not a clear photo). If the side cable doesn’t fall into the rain gutter by itself while raising the top, then tighten the zip tie some more. Overlap the holes if you need to.

![Fabric flap and nylon strap routing](../images/convertible-top-installation/72-strap-and-flap-routing.jpg)

![Additional view of strap routing](../images/convertible-top-installation/73-strap-routing-additional-view.jpg)

This is the cross-cable. Make sure it’s installed before you cover everything up by installing the headliner flap.

![Cross-cable before the headliner flap is installed](../images/convertible-top-installation/74-cross-cable.jpg)

Tip: There is a trick to assembling the forward sides. The forward spring on the big “rain gutter” cable must be screwed into the top from above.

These photos show the spring and screw.

![Forward spring and screw for the side cable](../images/convertible-top-installation/75-forward-spring-and-cable.jpg)

The cable must be tucked under the metal tab, and the tab bent back down. To reach the forward spring screw, you really need to have the whole forward piece of material loose (unhooked from the forward corners). To unhook the forward corners, you need to have the cable free (not trapped under the tab). So….

1. With the forward ⅓ of the top loose (not hooked over bow #1), fold the material back for easy access to the forward attachment for the spring/cable.
2. Screw down the forward end of the spring/cable
3. Pull the material forward and hook the corners over the ends of bow #1
4. Tuck the cable under the metal tab, and bend it down with a small hammer

The 2 elastic loops that are sewn to the top (by the door jambs) are not used. At least on any tops I’ve ever installed. I just cut them off so they don’t get snagged on the frame as it folds.

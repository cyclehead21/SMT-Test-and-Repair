## 2. Torn Seals and cracked flanges

The original vinyl material gets very brittle as it ages - for some reason.

When water leaks past the bag, it will fill the area beneath the cubby storage (where the evaporation reclamation canister is mounted) and (somehow) get down to the floorboards and under the seats.

![Drain area viewed from the cubby storage space](../images/drain-bag-side-gate-seal-repair/01-drain-area-from-cubby.jpg)

![Second view of the drain area from inside the cabin](../images/drain-bag-side-gate-seal-repair/02-drain-area-second-view.jpg)

I’ve also seen the plastic flange that attaches to the fabric bag get cracked. I think the flange may be preloaded during installation and cracks with age.

![Cracked plastic drain flange](../images/drain-bag-side-gate-seal-repair/03-cracked-drain-flange.png)

## 3. Finding leaks

You can hold a flashlight inside the bag, and look underneath from the cubby storage area for light shining through. Also, you can hold a flashlight in the cubby area and peek into the bag from above (with the ragtop in the “up” position). Or you can feel around for a tear in the bag material. Be gentle poking around. The vinyl material gets very brittle with age, and you don’t want to create problems.

## 4. Repair options

Proper installation of new “side gate seals” will require complete removal of the ragtop and ragtop frame. However, it is possible to install a trimmed-down portion of a new bag without completely removing the frame.

### Access

The plastic drain can be seen from outside if you remove the side vent trim. The three plastic clips are always hard and brittle.

[Link](<https://docs.google.com/document/d/1pM4HNBU6C8sgJqhfZbBbeiIaoB78B__JGMhfQeVIpFs/edit?usp=drivesdk>)

![Access with the side-vent trim removed](../images/drain-bag-side-gate-seal-repair/04-side-vent-access.jpg)

The flapper door piece just presses over the drainpipe.

![Drain flapper at the outside outlet](../images/drain-bag-side-gate-seal-repair/05-drain-flapper.png)

![Back of the removed flapper fitting](../images/drain-bag-side-gate-seal-repair/06-removed-flapper-back.jpg)

![Drain outlet with the flapper removed](../images/drain-bag-side-gate-seal-repair/07-drain-outlet.png)

![Access to the drain bag from inside the cubby area](../images/drain-bag-side-gate-seal-repair/08-inside-cubby-access.jpg)

You can gain some access by working from below by removing the cubby storage plastic panels. However you really need better access. You can unbolt the sides of the ragtop (only), just aft of the doors. Then flip the ragtop sides up and out of the way for better access. You’ll need to remove the curved bracket shown in photos below.

### Repairs

If you’re lucky and only the plastic flange has cracked, some epoxy may be sufficient to stop the leaks. The plastic grill is removable. Just pry it out of the plastic fitting. The plastic grill is not really necessary. Feel free to leave it off.

![Removed plastic drain grille](../images/drain-bag-side-gate-seal-repair/09-removable-drain-grille.png)

If the bag material is torn, then it gets more complicated. I have made homemade repairs to the torn bags by cutting off the brittle cracked material. Then making a new bag using new vinyl material, and mounting the old plastic drain piece to the new bag. I install the new bag outside and below the old bag, so water will drain properly.

I’ve made new bags by (a) stitching, and by (b) gluing and taping with double sided “carpet tape”. Both methods work fine.

### Repair Materials

- Double sided carpet tape
- DAP Weldwood Contact Cement
- Vinyl fabric

### Disassembly

1. Remove 10 mm nuts around the back corners of the cabin
2. Remove the large curved bracket
3. Remove hidden 10 mm nut (photo)
4. Remove hidden 10 mm bolt (photo)
5. Peel velcro flap loose and unthread from ragtop frame
6. Fold the side of the ragtop up and out of the way for access
